# 1763 North American Interior: Population & Political Control

Research compilation for historical game map (Feb 1763). Population figures in thousands; game conversion = 4000 people per map unit.

---

## 1. GREAT LAKES REGION
**Territory**: Michigan, Wisconsin, Ohio Country, Illinois Country  
**Indigenous Population (c.1760)**: ~75,000–100,000

### Major Nations & Estimates
| Nation/Confederacy | Population | Notes |
|-------------------|-----------|-------|
| Anishinaabe (Ojibwe/Ojibwa) | 25,000–35,000 | Dominant around Superior/Michigan; Richter notes expansion into former Huron lands |
| Odawa (Ottawa) | 8,000–10,000 | Key intermediaries in fur trade; Pontiac's people |
| Potawatomi | 4,000–6,000 | Southern Michigan/Illinois |
| Miami Confederacy | 4,500–6,000 | Wabash/Maumee valleys; reduced by mid-1700s wars |
| Illinois Confederacy | 2,000–3,000 | Severely reduced from ~10k in 1680 (Calloway) |
| Wyandot (Huron) | 1,500–2,000 | Remnants; Detroit/Sandusky |
| Shawnee | 3,000–4,000 | Ohio Country; mobile/migrating |
| Ho-Chunk (Winnebago) | 3,000–4,000 | Wisconsin |
| Menominee | 3,000–4,000 | Wisconsin/Michigan |

### European Presence (Feb 1763)
- **French posts** (about to transfer): Detroit (~800–2,000 total including Canadiens, soldiers, voyageurs), Fort Michilimackinac (~500), Vincennes (~100–200), Kaskaskia (~1,000), Fort de Chartres, Green Bay
- **British**: Virtually none yet; garrisons won't arrive until summer 1763
- **Total European**: ~3,000–4,000

### Political Control (Feb 1763)
**STATUS: CONTESTED — French ceded to Britain on paper (Treaty of Paris, Feb 10 1763), but Indigenous control remains**

- Treaty of Paris (signed Feb 10, 1763) transfers French claims east of Mississippi to Britain
- **HOWEVER**: Pontiac's War (May 1763–1766) demonstrates Indigenous rejection of this transfer
- Calloway (*The Scratch of a Pen*, 2006): "Indians did not know, or did not accept, that they had been conquered" (p. 7); the pays d'en haut remained Indigenous country where Europeans lived "on sufferance" (p. 92)
- Royal Proclamation (Oct 1763) will recognize this by forbidding settlement west of Appalachians
- White (*The Middle Ground*, 1991): French-Indigenous alliance system still intact; British authority non-existent outside forts

**MAP RECOMMENDATION**: 
- Option A: Mark as British (legal ownership per treaty) but expect it to flip to Indigenous revolt immediately
- Option B: Mark as Indigenous-controlled (de facto reality) with British "claims"
- **PREFERRED**: Indigenous ownership. The treaty was signed in Europe without Indigenous consent; Pontiac's War proves no British control exists.

**Sources**: Calloway 2006, White 1991, Dowd (*War Under Heaven*, 2002), Richter (*Facing East*, 2001)

## 2. UPPER MISSISSIPPI / "GREAT FORESTS"
**Territory**: Minnesota, northern Wisconsin, Ontario border lakes  
**Indigenous Population (c.1760)**: ~25,000–30,000

### Major Nations
| Nation | Population | Notes |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| Dakota/Santee Sioux | 15,000–20,000 | Eastern/Woodland Dakota (Mdewakanton, Wahpekute, Sisseton, Wahpeton) |
| Ojibwe (western bands) | 8,000–10,000 | Pushing west from Superior, displacing Dakota (White 1978) |
| Cree (southern bands) | 2,000–3,000 | Northern fringe, fur trade intermediaries |

### European Presence
- Essentially NONE in interior
- French voyageurs/coureurs de bois seasonally (no permanent posts north of Michilimackinac)
- Ray (*Indians in the Fur Trade*, 1974): HBC posts stay on Hudson Bay; interior not penetrated until 1780s–1790s

### Political Control (Feb 1763)
**STATUS: INDIGENOUS OWNERSHIP — No European territorial control**

- Nominally part of French cession to Britain, but France never controlled this region
- White ("The Winning of the West", 1978): Ojibwe-Dakota wars for wild rice lands ongoing; European power irrelevant
- No posts, no trade monopoly, no governance

**MAP RECOMMENDATION**: UNOWNED (Indigenous)

**Sources**: White 1978, Ray 1974, Hickerson (*The Chippewa and Their Neighbors*, 1970)

## 3. GREAT PLAINS
**Territory**: Dakotas, Nebraska, Kansas, Oklahoma, Texas panhandle, eastern Montana/Wyoming  
**Indigenous Population (c.1760)**: ~150,000–200,000

### Major Nations & Estimates
| Nation/Alliance | Population | Notes |
|----------------|-----------|-------|
| Comanche (Numunuu) | 20,000–30,000 | Hämäläinen (*Comanche Empire*, 2008): dominant power of southern plains by 1750s |
| Lakota/Western Sioux | 15,000–25,000 | Expanding west; Hämäläinen (*Lakota America*, 2019): militarized horse culture |
| Pawnee Confederacy | 10,000–12,000 | Nebraska; semi-sedentary farmers + buffalo hunters |
| Cheyenne | 3,500–4,500 | Transitioning to plains horse culture |
| Arapaho | 3,000–4,000 | Allied with Cheyenne |
| Kiowa | 2,000–3,000 | Southern plains; will ally with Comanche |
| Omaha | 2,800–3,500 | Missouri River; semi-sedentary |
| Ponca | 800–1,000 | Missouri River |
| Osage | 6,000–7,000 | Missouri/Arkansas; powerful middlemen |
| Wichita Confederacy | 3,000–4,000 | Kansas/Oklahoma; Comanche allies |
| Plains Apache (Kiowa-Apache, Lipan) | 3,000–5,000 | Fragmented by Comanche expansion |
| Kansa (Kaw) | 1,500–2,000 | Kansas River |
| Mandan | 3,000–4,000 | Upper Missouri; smallpox not yet (1781) |
| Hidatsa | 2,500–3,000 | Upper Missouri |
| Arikara | 3,000–4,000 | Missouri River |
| Crow (Apsáalooke) | 4,000–5,000 | Montana/Wyoming; recently split from Hidatsa |
| Blackfoot Confederacy | 15,000–20,000 | Northern plains (Montana/Alberta); see also Canada section |

### European Presence (Feb 1763)
- Spanish New Mexico: some contact with Comanche (raids), but no posts on plains
- French: occasional traders reached Mandan/Arikara villages (La Vérendrye 1738), but no permanent presence
- **Total European on plains proper**: <100 transients

### Political Control (Feb 1763)
**STATUS: INDIGENOUS OWNERSHIP — Comancheria and Lakota territory**

- Hämäläinen (2008): "Comanche empire" controls southern plains from Arkansas River to Rio Grande; Spain negotiates with Comanche as equals (peace treaty not until 1786)
- Hämäläinen (2019): Lakota expanding into northern plains; will dominate by 1800
- Spanish claim from Louisiana (west of Mississippi) and New Mexico, but DuVal (*The Native Ground*, 2006): "Spain's failure to control… left most of the Southwest in the hands of autonomous native polities" (p. 6)
- Louisiana Purchase (1803) will transfer these "claims" to US, but Indigenous control remains until 1860s–1890s

**MAP RECOMMENDATION**: UNOWNED (Indigenous). Spanish/French claims are paper fictions.

**Sources**: Hämäläinen 2008, 2019; DuVal 2006; Isenberg (*The Destruction of the Bison*, 2000); West (*The Contested Plains*, 1998)

**Indigenous Population (c.1760)**: ~150,000–200,000

### Major Nations (Estimates Pre-Contact, Less Decline Than Plains)
| Region/Group | Population | Notes |

**NONE**

### Political Control (Feb 1763)
**STATUS: INDIGENOUS OWNERSHIP — Zero European contact**

- No European has set foot in interior; coastal contact minimal/indirect
- Gibson (*Otter Skins, Boston Ships*, 1992): maritime fur trade begins 1780s

**MAP RECOMMENDATION**: UNOWNED (Indigenous)

**Sources**: Boyd 1999, Gibson 1992, Ames & Maschner (*Peoples of the Northwest Coast*, 1999)

## 6. CALIFORNIA
**Territory**: California (entire state)  
**Indigenous Population (c.1760)**: ~250,000–310,000

### Population Density & Groups
- **Highest population density north of Mesoamerica**: Cook (*The Population of the California Indians 1769–1970*, 1976) estimates 310,000 at Spanish contact (1769)
- Six years BEFORE Spanish missions, so 1763 population likely similar or slightly higher

| Region | Population | Major Groups |
|--------|-----------|--------------|
| Central Coast | 60,000–70,000 | Chumash (~18k), Salinan, Esselen, Ohlone/Costanoan (~10k) |
| San Francisco Bay | 25,000–30,000 | Ohlone, Coast Miwok, Patwin |
| Sacramento Valley | 50,000–60,000 | Patwin, Maidu, Wintun, Yana |
| San Joaquin Valley | 40,000–50,000 | Yokuts (~18k), Miwok |
| Southern California | 30,000–40,000 | Tongva/Gabrielino, Luiseño, Cahuilla, Serrano, Kumeyaay |
| North Coast | 25,000–30,000 | Pomo (~8k), Yuki, Hupa, Yurok, Karuk, Tolowa |
| Northeast | 15,000–20,000 | Modoc, Achomawi, Atsugewi, Shasta |
| Desert (Mojave/Colorado River) | 5,000–10,000 | Mojave, Quechan, Chemehuevi |

### European Presence (Feb 1763)
**NONE**

- Spanish missions do not start until **1769** (Mission San Diego de Alcalá, July 16, 1769)
- Serra founds first mission 6+ years AFTER Feb 1763
- Portolà expedition (1769) is first Spanish overland entry
- Manila galleons sail offshore (1565–1815) but don't land

### Political Control (Feb 1763)
**STATUS: INDIGENOUS OWNERSHIP — Entirely unconquered**

- Spanish claim California on maps (exploration of Cabrillo 1542, Vizcaíno 1602), but zero presence
- Hackel (*Children of Coyote, Missionaries of Saint Francis*, 2005): "Before 1769, California Indians lived without any European presence" (p. 23)

**MAP RECOMMENDATION**: UNOWNED (Indigenous). Spanish missions are 6 years in the future.

**Sources**: Cook 1976, Hackel 2005, Lightfoot (*Indians, Missionaries, and Merchants*, 2005)

## 7. AMERICAN SOUTHWEST
**Territory**: New Mexico, Arizona, west Texas, parts of Colorado/Utah  
**Indigenous Population (c.1760)**: ~60,000–70,000

### Major Nations & Estimates
| Nation | Population | Notes |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| Pueblo peoples (19 pueblos in 1760) | 9,000–11,000 | Under Spanish rule; pre-contact ~60k, reduced by revolt (1680) + epidemics. Gutiérrez 1991 |
| Navajo (Diné) | 15,000–20,000 | Independent; sheep pastoralism + raiding; expanding |
| Western Apache bands (Chiricahua, Mimbres, Coyotero, etc.) | 8,000–10,000 | Apacheria = independent; raid NM/Sonora |
| Jicarilla Apache | 2,000–3,000 | NE New Mexico |
| Mescalero Apache | 2,000–3,000 | SE New Mexico |
| Lipan Apache | 3,000–4,000 | West Texas; pressured by Comanche |
| Hopi pueblos | 2,500–3,000 | Independent (never reconquered after 1680); Arizona |
| Pima (Akimel O'odham) | 3,000–4,000 | Sonora/Arizona; some mission contact |
| Tohono O'odham (Papago) | 6,000–8,000 | Sonora/Arizona |
| Yuma (Quechan) | 3,000–4,000 | Colorado River |
| Havasupai / Hualapai / Yavapai | 2,500–3,500 | Arizona |

### European Presence (Feb 1763)
- **Spanish New Mexico** (since 1598): Santa Fe (~2,000 españoles + genízaros), Albuquerque (~1,000), El Paso (~1,500), scattered ranchos. Total Spanish/mestizo population ~10,000–12,000
- **Missions**: Franciscan missions at Rio Grande pueblos (~2,000 Pueblo under mission system)
- **Presidios**: Santa Fe, Albuquerque, El Paso garrison maybe 200–300 soldiers total
- Spanish Sonora/Arizona: Tubac presidio (1752), Tumacácori mission, but minimal presence

### Political Control (Feb 1763)
**STATUS: MIXED — Spanish control Rio Grande corridor only; rest is Indigenous (Apacheria, Navajo, Comanche raiding zone)**

- **Spanish-controlled**: Rio Grande valley from El Paso to Taos; 19 Pueblos under nominal Spanish rule (though Gutiérrez 1991 shows Pueblo autonomy within missions)
- **Independent Indigenous**: 
  - Apacheria (Chiricahua, Mimbres, etc.): all of southern NM/Arizona
  - Navajo: northwest NM/northeast AZ
  - Hopi: independent in Arizona (refused Spanish rule after 1680)
  - Comanche raids from plains: Spanish sign peace 1786 (not yet in 1763)
- Weber (*The Spanish Frontier in North America*, 1992): "Spain held only a fragile foothold" in New Mexico (p. 188); "Apaches controlled more of the Southwest than did Spaniards" (p. 193)

**MAP RECOMMENDATION**: 
- Spanish own: Rio Grande corridor (Santa Fe, Albuquerque, El Paso areas)
- Indigenous own: Everything else (Apacheria, Navajo, Hopi, Comanche-raided plains fringe)

**Sources**: Weber 1992, Gutiérrez (*When Jesus Came, the Corn Mothers Went Away*, 1991), Brooks (*Captives and Cousins*, 2002), Hämäläinen 2008

## 8. CANADIAN WEST & NORTH
**Territory**: British Columbia, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba (west of HBC posts), NWT, Yukon, Nunavut  
**Indigenous Population (c.1760)**: ~120,000–180,000 (huge area, sparse)

### Major Nations & Estimates
| Nation/Region | Population | Notes |
|--------------|-----------|-------|
| Cree (Plains + Woodland bands) | 30,000–40,000 | Dominant middlemen in fur trade; Ray 1974 |
| Blackfoot Confederacy (Siksika, Kainai, Piikani) | 15,000–20,000 | Southern Alberta/Montana; horse warriors |
| Assiniboine | 8,000–10,000 | Saskatchewan/Manitoba; Cree allies |
| Dene/Chipewyan (Athabaskan groups) | 15,000–20,000 | NWT/northern Alberta; subarctic hunters |
| Inuit (Copper, Caribou, Netsilik, etc.) | 20,000–25,000 | Arctic coast; Nunavut |
| Tlingit | 10,000–15,000 | SE Alaska / BC coast panhandle |
| Haida | 8,000–10,000 | Haida Gwaii (Queen Charlotte Islands) |
| Tsimshian | 8,000–10,000 | BC north coast |
| Kwakwaka'wakw (Kwakiutl) | 8,000–10,000 | Vancouver Island north |
| Nuu-chah-nulth (Nootka) | 6,000–8,000 | Vancouver Island west coast |
| Coast Salish (Straits, Halkomelem, etc.) | 15,000–20,000 | Vancouver Island south / Fraser River |
| Interior Salish (Secwepemc/Shuswap, St'at'imc/Lillooet, Nlaka'pamux/Thompson, Okanagan) | 15,000–20,000 | BC interior |
| Sekani, Carrier (Dakelh), Chilcotin | 8,000–10,000 | BC interior Athabaskan |

### European Presence (Feb 1763)
- **Hudson's Bay Company** (chartered 1670): 
  - Posts ON Hudson Bay only: York Factory, Churchill, Moose Factory, Albany, etc.
  - Ray (*Indians in the Fur Trade*, 1974): HBC traders stay at bayside posts; Cree middlemen bring furs from interior
  - **Interior expansion not until 1770s–1790s** (Cumberland House 1774 is first inland post)
- **Pacific coast**: ZERO contact (Cook 1778 not yet; Spanish Pérez 1774 reaches Haida Gwaii)
- **Total HBC personnel**: ~300–500 at Bay posts

### Political Control (Feb 1763)
**STATUS: INDIGENOUS OWNERSHIP, except nominal HBC charter claim to "Rupert's Land"**

- **Rupert's Land**: HBC charter (1670) claims all land draining into Hudson Bay (modern Manitoba, most of Saskatchewan/Alberta, parts of NWT/Ontario/Quebec/Minnesota/North Dakota)
  - **BUT**: Ray 1974, Rich (*The Fur Trade and the Northwest*, 1967): HBC has zero control beyond musket-shot of posts; Cree and Assiniboine control trade and territory
  - "Home Guard Cree" live near posts; interior nations are independent
- **British Columbia / Pacific coast**: Not even claimed by Britain yet (sovereignty disputes with Spain until Nootka Convention 1790)
- **Arctic**: Inuit independent; no European contact except rare HBC encounters at Churchill

**MAP RECOMMENDATION**: 
- UNOWNED (Indigenous) for all interior, BC, Arctic
- Optional: Mark Hudson Bay coast as HBC if you want to show the charter claim, but note it's a paper claim

**Sources**: Ray 1974, Rich 1967, Boyd 1999 (for BC), Morrison & Wilson (*Native Peoples: The Canadian Experience*, 2004)

## 9. ALASKA
**Territory**: Alaska (entire state)  
**Indigenous Population (c.1760)**: ~70,000–90,000

### Major Nations & Estimates
| Group | Population | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| Inuit (Iñupiat, Yup'ik, Cup'ik) | 30,000–40,000 | Arctic/Bering coast; north + west Alaska |
| Aleut (Unangan) | 12,000–15,000 | Aleutian Islands; pre-Russian contact; Russian fur hunters arrive 1741, devastate population |
| Tlingit | 10,000–15,000 | SE Alaska panhandle; see also Canada section |
| Athabaskan (Dena'ina, Ahtna, Tanana, Gwich'in, etc.) | 12,000–15,000 | Interior Alaska |
| Eyak, Haida (Kaigani) | 1,000–2,000 | SE Alaska coast (small groups) |

### European Presence (Feb 1763)
- **Russian promyshlenniki** (fur hunters): 
  - Bering expedition (1741) reached Alaska
  - Private hunters in **Aleutian Islands from 1740s–1760s** (seasonal fur hunting expeditions)
  - **NO permanent Russian settlement until 1784** (Kodiak/Three Saints Bay)
  - Black (*Russians in Alaska*, 2004): 1760s = scattered hunting parties, brutal enslavement of Aleuts for sea otter hunting, but no colonial administration
- Estimated Russians in Aleutians (1763): ~200–400 seasonal hunters (not permanent)

### Political Control (Feb 1763)
**STATUS: INDIGENOUS OWNERSHIP — Russian claim emerging but no settlement/control**

- Russian hunting parties operate in Aleutians, but this is extractive raiding, not territorial control
- Aleut population will collapse from ~15k (1740) to ~2k (1800) due to Russian violence + disease (Black 2004)
- **Permanent Russian settlement**: not until 1784 (Kodiak), then 1799 Russian-American Company (Sitka)
- In Feb 1763: Russian hunters present but not governing; mainland Alaska entirely Indigenous

**MAP RECOMMENDATION**: UNOWNED (Indigenous). You could mark Aleutians as "Russian presence" if you want to show fur-hunting activity, but no Russian sovereignty yet.

**Sources**: Black 2004, Gibson (*Feeding the Russian Fur Trade*, 1969), Crowell et al. (*Living Our Cultures, Sharing Our Heritage: The First Peoples of Alaska*, 2010)

## CRITICAL CLARIFICATIONS FOR FEB 1763 MAP

### 1. Treaty of Paris (Feb 10, 1763) vs. Indigenous Reality
The treaty transferred French claims to Britain, but:
- Indigenous nations were not parties to the treaty
- Pontiac's War (May 1763) immediately contested British claims
- Royal Proclamation (Oct 1763) will acknowledge Indigenous land rights west of Appalachians
- **Recommendation**: Show Indigenous ownership for Great Lakes; British control is a legal fiction

### 2. California = Entirely Indigenous in Feb 1763
- Spanish missions do not begin until **July 1769** (San Diego)
- Any map showing Spanish California in 1763 is anachronistic by 6 years
- Population is very high (~310k per Cook) and entirely Native

### 3. Plains = Independent Indigenous Polities
- Comanche Empire (Hämäläinen) controls southern plains
- Lakota expanding into northern plains
- Spanish/French claims are paper only; no posts, no control
- These nations are sovereign powers negotiating with European empires as equals

### 4. HBC "Rupert's Land" = Paper Claim
- Charter (1670) claims Hudson Bay watershed
- But HBC has zero interior presence in 1763
- Cree and Assiniboine middlemen control trade
- Interior posts not built until 1770s–1790s

### 5. Alaska = Russian Presence but Not Sovereignty
- Russian hunters in Aleutians (1740s–1760s) but no permanent settlement until 1784
- Brutal fur-hunting raids, not colonial administration
- Mainland Alaska has zero Russian contact
