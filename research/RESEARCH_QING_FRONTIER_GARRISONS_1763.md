# Qing Frontier / Subject Garrisons c.1763 — Research Digest

For the OOB decision (BT-33/34): which Qing frontier/subject territories had STANDING garrisons at 1763 vs arm's-length tributaries. KEY DISTINCTION: **藩部** (Lifan-Yuan-administered Inner-Asian dependencies, under ambans/generals — GARRISONED) vs **朝貢國** (external tributary states, tribute+investiture only — NO Qing troops).

Garrison YES/NO + 藩部-vs-朝貢國 distinction = CONFIRMED. TROOP COUNTS remain SOFT — for an authoritative build re-verify counts vs Millward *Beyond the Pass* (Xinjiang), a 駐防制度 monograph, and 欽定大清會典.

## VERIFIED (browsing agent, zh/en.wikipedia)
- **Tibet** amban 駐藏大臣 PERMANENT from **1727** (雍正五年); Green Standard rotated from **Chengdu 成都** on a **3-year 換防** cycle; the 1750 amban killings + 1751 善後章程 predate 1763, so the two-amban+garrison system is firmly in place by 1763. **Lhasa garrison size = "more than 1,300" Green Standard — ACADEMICALLY CONFIRMED: Dabringhaus 2014 p.123 (citing Dai 2009).** In the mod's OOB company-scale this = size≈2.
- **1763 amban COMMANDERS** (清史稿-derived 年表): Lhasa = 傅景 Fujing (Fuca, Manchu Bordered Yellow, 1761-65); Xining = 七十五 Qishiwu (Guwalgiya, Manchu Plain Yellow, 1763-66) [+ 容保 Rongbao 1762-63 the exact-date incumbent]. Both Manchu → culture=manchu/mahayana. In OOB as char:610/611 (commit dd38d4057).
- **Ili** General 伊犁將軍 est. **1762**, first holder **明瑞 Mingrui** (out 1762/11). Mixed 満營 (惠遠/惠寧) + 綠營 cities + redeployed 錫伯/索倫/察哈爾/厄魯特 battalions. Troop TOTAL (~20-30k) plausible but NOT numerically sourced.
- **Mongolia:** Uliastai 定邊左副將軍 est. 1733 commands **Mongol banner levies**; own Green Standard = a handful of rotating officers only. 庫倫辦事大臣 est. 1761. Minimal-Qing-garrison / native-levy model.
- **Qinghai:** 西寧辦事大臣 est. 1724 (29 Mongol banners + 40 Tibetan tribes, 駐防 duties).
- **Manchuria:** 盛京/吉林/黑龍江將軍 Eight-Banner garrisons.
- **YUNNAN 雲南:** a full PROVINCE (行省), NOT 藩部. 雲貴總督 + 提督 command the 雲貴綠營 (no bannermen). Green Standard ~40,000 on establishment / ~20,000 effective (清緬戰爭 article). Sino-Burmese War **1765–69** fought from Yunnan (buildup ~1763). Ortai 鄂爾泰 gaitu-guiliu 改土歸流 from 1726 abolished ~47 Yunnan tusi; residual 土司 remain. → normal provincial Green-Standard garrison, not a Lifan-Yuan amban garrison.
- **Tributaries** handled by 禮部 (Board of Rites), NOT Lifan Yuan → tribute+investiture only, ungarrisoned. Nepal NOT tributary until after the 1792 Gurkha war. Burma unstable (war 1765-69); Siam/Ayutthaya fell to Burma 1767.

## GARRISONED (藩部) — give an OOB garrison
- **Tibet 西藏**: YES ~1,300 (confirmed) Green Standard rotated from Sichuan + small banner contingent. Cmd = two co-equal ambans 駐藏大臣. (BT-33.)
- **Xinjiang/Ili 新疆/伊犁**: YES ~20,000–30,000. Mixed Eight Banners + Green Standard, 換防 rotation. Cmd = Ili General + 參贊大臣/都統 at Ürümqi/Kashgar/Yarkand.
- **Qinghai 青海**: YES ~3,000–5,000. Green Standard + Qinghai-Mongol levies. Cmd = Xining amban.
- **Manchuria/Northeast 東三省**: YES ~50,000–80,000 Eight Banners (banner homeland). Cmd = three generals.

## PARTIAL / nuanced
- **Mongolia 蒙古**: MINIMAL standing Qing garrison (~1,000–2,000 at Uliastai / Khüree); BULK = Mongol league-banner levies 盟旗兵 under hereditary 札薩克 princes. OOB rec: small Qing garrison + larger native levy pool.

## NOT GARRISONED (朝貢國) — NO Qing units on their soil (correct as-is)
- Korea 朝鮮, Vietnam 安南, Ryukyu 琉球, Siam 暹羅, Burma 緬甸, Sulu, Laos — sovereign tributaries: tribute + investiture 冊封 only. Nepal NOT yet tributary in 1763.

## SINO-BURMESE WAR (1765-69) QING DEPLOYMENT
(POST-1763, does NOT change the shipped 1763 OOB — reference for future Yunnan/Burma content.) Fought from Yunnan Green Standard base. Four campaigns: (1) 1765 ~6,000, repelled; (2) 1766-67 ~27,500 (14,000 GS), failed; (3) 1767-68 ~70,000 (30,000 bannermen/Mongols + 12,000 GS + Yunnan GS), cmd **Ming Rui 明瑞** — main army annihilated at Maymyo, ~92% casualties, Ming Rui hanged himself; (4) 1769 ~60,000 (40,000 bannermen + 20,000 GS/militia), cmd **Fuheng 傅恆**, Fujian navy sailors + carpenter regiment, heavy disease losses, truce. Disease a major factor (winter campaigning to reduce malaria). Sources: Harvey 1925 *History of Burma* pp.333-35 (~55-60k Qing); Myint-U 2006 pp.102-03; Giersch 2006 *Asian Borderlands*; Whiting 2002 *Imperial Chinese Military History* pp.480-81.
