# Qing peasant cost of living, mid-Qianlong (~1763) — sourced research digest (2026-08-09)

## HEADLINE FIGURE (recommended plausibility anchor)

**Bare-bones annual subsistence, single adult, Beijing, ~1750 ≈ 4.9 taels/year**
- Allen, Bassino, Ma, Moll-Murata, van Zanden, "Wages, Prices, and Living Standards in China,
  1738–1925," *Economic History Review* 64:S1 (2011), Table 5 p.25: Beijing bare-bones basket = **182.6 g
  silver/person/year**; ÷ 37 g/tael (their fixed conversion, fn.6 p.9) = **≈ 4.9 taels/yr**.
- Daily: 4.9 ÷ 365 ≈ **0.0134 taels/day** ≈ **12 wén/day** (at ~900 wén/tael).
- Family of four (paper's own method: ×1.05 rent, ×3): ≈ **15.5 taels/yr** (Beijing, derived from their
  stated inputs — not directly quoted).
- **Rival sourced reconstruction:** Yangzi Delta family bare-bones **22.59 taels** 1745–54 (Pomeranz/Li,
  cited in Allen et al. p.30). A genuine documented regional DISAGREEMENT — not an error. Beijing-basket
  ~15.5 vs Delta ~22.59 brackets the family figure.

## Supporting sourced figures

**Grain (rice) price, mid-Qianlong ≈ 1.0–1.5 taels/shih** (rising to 1.5–2.0 by 1770s–90s).
- PRIMARY anchor: Huang Ang 黄印, observed **1753** Wu-hsi (near Soochow) normal rice = **1.5 taels/shih**,
  up from 1.0 pre-Yongzheng (via Wang Yeh-chien, "The Secular Trend of Prices during the Ch'ing Period,"
  *J. Inst. Chinese Studies CUHK* 9:2 (1972), p.351 n.6). Nanchang (Kiangsi) 0.9 tael/shih in Yongzheng →
  1.5–1.7 by century-end (p.354). Secondary Chinese cross-check (~1.4 taels/shih mid-Qianlong) consistent.

**Unskilled daily wage, mid-to-late Qianlong ≈ 0.04–0.10 taels/day** (Beijing/capital high end).
- Allen et al. Table 1 p.12 (from *Wuliao jiazhi zeli* 物料價值則例, 1769, Qing govt schedule): Beijing
  **0.077 taels/day**; national pop-weighted avg **0.044**; regression-baseline Beijing 0.0897; Canton VOC
  port labour 0.08–0.10. NB a regulated wage FLOOR, not necessarily market pay (paper's own caution p.13).

**Silver–copper rate, ~1763 ≈ 700–1,000 wén/tael** (official parity 1,000; market drifted).
- Wang 1972 p.348 n.3: govt parity 1 tael = 1,000 wén but "hardly followed in the market"; implies rate
  moved ~700 → ~1,400 wén/tael over 1736–95 (p.351), so 1763 sat ~700–1,000. Chen Chao-nan (1966), the
  authoritative Yongzheng-Qianlong silver-cash study, cited by Wang but NOT directly accessible (gap).

**Grain subsistence RATION (no clean single sourced number):** ~3 shi/yr (Bao Shichen, but 1820s,
off-period) ↔ ~5.5 shi (Shen-shi Nongshu, labouring man) ↔ 780 jin/capita GRAIN-POSSESSION (Wu Hui 1993,
a supply-side aggregate incl. seed/feed/tax, NOT a consumption norm; primary text not accessed). Famine-
relief doles (1.5 dou/month adult) are emergency levels, below normal subsistence — not usable as ordinary.

**Welfare ratio (wage ÷ family bare-bones cost), Beijing 1738 ≈ 1.6–1.7** (Allen et al. Fig.5 p.27),
declining to ~1.0 by the Taiping era. WR=1 ⇒ full-time work exactly covers bare-bones family subsistence.

## Internal consistency check (independent sources agree)
4.9 taels/yr ÷ 1.5 taels/shih ≈ 3.3 shih rice/yr — inside the (weak) 3–5.5 shih ration range. Allen's
basket cost and Wang's price series were built independently yet reconcile → the ~5 taels/adult/yr,
~15–22 taels/family/yr anchor is robust.

## Use in #23 (see design/AUDIT_CURRENCY_23.md acceptance criteria)
The fixed `CURRENCY_essentials_buying_power` is a basket price ÷ the silver peg. Its steady-state
tael-equivalent should land in the **single-adult ~5 taels/yr / family ~15–22 taels/yr** ballpark (or a
defensible game-scaled multiple of it). A steady-state that implies a cost of living orders of magnitude
off that — a peasant "spending" hundreds of taels/yr, or a fraction of a tael — means the trade-price→peg
SCALE is mis-calibrated, which is part of what #23 must correct. This is the yardstick, NOT a mechanic input.
