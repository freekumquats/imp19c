## OWNERSHIP (political control, Feb 1763)

### GREAT BRITAIN
**Territorial extent**: England, Wales, Scotland, Ireland (entire island under British control post-1691 Williamite War).  
**Capital**: London  
**Key cities**: Edinburgh, Dublin, Bristol, Liverpool, Manchester, Birmingham  
**Subject/overlord/personal-union**: Hanover in personal union (George III both King of Britain and Elector of Hanover; governed via Privy Council of Hanover & German Chancery in St James's Palace; George III never visited Hanover).  
**Source**: Black 2001 [MONOGRAPH]; Wikipedia [WEAK] personal-union mechanics.  
**ANACHRONISM FLAGS**: None. Acts of Union 1707 united England-Wales with Scotland; Ireland under English/British rule since 1691.

### HANOVER (Electorate of Brunswick-Lüneburg)
**Territorial extent**: Principality of Calenberg, Göttingen, Grubenhagen, Lüneburg (electoral dignity 1692).  
**Capital**: Hanover  
**Overlord**: Personal union with Great Britain (George III, 1760–1820).  
**Governance**: Privy Council of Hanover (*Geheimer Rat*) held executive authority; German Chancery (*Deutsche Kanzlei*) in London transmitted memoranda. Premiere Minister 1753–1770: Gerlach Adolph, Baron von Münchhausen.  
**Source**: Wikipedia [WEAK]; constitutional structure.  
**ANACHRONISM FLAGS**: None. Personal union ends 1837 (Salic Law separation upon Victoria's accession).

### FRANCE (Bourbon)
**Territorial extent**: Roughly modern France minus Corsica (Genoese until 1768), minus Lorraine (autonomous under Stanisław Leszczyński until 1766, then French), minus Savoy/Nice (Savoyard). **Includes** Alsace (acquired 1648–81), Franche-Comté.  
**Capital**: Versailles/Paris  
**Key cities**: Lyon, Marseille, Bordeaux, Rouen, Strasbourg, Lille  
**Ruler**: Louis XV (1715–1774, age 52 in 1763).  
**Foreign Minister**: César Gabriel de Choiseul, Duke of Praslin (signed Treaty of Paris 10 Feb 1763).  
**Source**: Doyle 1989 [MONOGRAPH]; Wikipedia [WEAK] ministerial dates.  
**ANACHRONISM FLAGS**:  
- **Corsica**: Must be Genoese in 1763; sold to France 1768.  
- **Lorraine**: Autonomous 1763 (Stanisław Leszczyński ex-King of Poland); French 1766.

### SPAIN (Bourbon)
**Territorial extent**: Iberian peninsula minus Portugal; Balearic Islands. Spanish Netherlands LOST to Austria 1714.  
**Capital**: Madrid  
**Key cities**: Barcelona, Seville, Valencia, Cádiz, Zaragoza  
**Ruler**: Charles III (r. 1759–88, age 46 in 1763); initiating Bourbon Reforms.  
**First Secretary of State**: Ricardo Wall (15 May 1754 – 9 Oct 1763; resigned 235 days after Treaty of Paris).  
**Source**: Lynch 1989 [MONOGRAPH]; Wikipedia [WEAK] ministerial dates.  
**ANACHRONISM FLAGS**: None. War of Spanish Succession settled Bourbon legitimacy but cost European possessions.

### PORTUGAL
**Territorial extent**: Modern Portugal, including Algarve.  
**Capital**: Lisbon  
**Key cities**: Porto, Coimbra, Évora  
**Ruler**: José I (1750–77, age 48 in 1763).  
**Chief Minister (de facto)**: Sebastião José de Carvalho e Melo, **Count of Oeiras** (title 1759; Marquis of Pombal from 1769/1770). Secretary of State for Internal Affairs (1756–1777).  
**Source**: Maxwell 1995 [MONOGRAPH]; Wikipedia [WEAK] title dates.  
**ANACHRONISM FLAGS**: None. Independent since 1640 (end of Iberian Union).

### DUTCH REPUBLIC (United Provinces)
**Territorial extent**: Seven provinces (Holland, Zeeland, Utrecht, Gelderland, Overijssel, Friesland, Groningen) + Generality Lands (Brabant, Limburg, Flanders areas).  
**Capital**: The Hague (seat of government); Amsterdam (largest city).  
**Key cities**: Rotterdam, Utrecht, Leiden, Haarlem, Groningen  
**Stadtholder**: William V, Prince of Orange (acceded 1751 at age 3; age 14–15 in Feb 1763; came of age 1766).  
**Regents (1763)**: Princess Marie Louise of Hesse-Kassel (1759–9 March 1765) + Duke Louis Ernest of Brunswick-Lüneburg (1759–1766 co-regent).  
**Source**: Israel 1995 [MONOGRAPH]; Wikipedia [WEAK] regency structure.  
**ANACHRONISM FLAGS**: None. **CRITICAL**: Austrian Netherlands = Habsburg (modern Belgium), NOT Dutch until 1815 Congress of Vienna.

### AUSTRIAN NETHERLANDS
**Territorial extent**: Modern Belgium and Luxembourg (under Habsburg personal union, NOT part of Dutch Republic).  
**Capital**: Brussels  
**Sovereign**: Maria Theresa, Archduchess of Austria, Queen of Hungary and Bohemia (1740–80, age 45 in 1763).  
**Governor-General**: Prince Charles Alexander of Lorraine (1744 – 4 July 1780); brother-in-law of Maria Theresa; "de facto sovereign" administrator.  
**Source**: Wikipedia [WEAK] Austrian Netherlands governance; Ingrao 2000 [MONOGRAPH] Habsburg territories.  
**ANACHRONISM FLAGS**: **CRITICAL**: Belgium/Luxembourg = AUSTRIAN not Dutch 1763. Incorporated into Kingdom of Netherlands only 1815 (Congress of Vienna).

### SWISS CONFEDERATION
**Territorial extent**: Thirteen cantons (core) + associated territories; roughly modern Switzerland **minus** Geneva (independent city-republic, not yet Swiss) & Neuchâtel (Prussian possession 1707–1848).  
**Capital**: None (confederation); Bern, Zürich, Luzern as major centers.  
**Key cities**: Basel, Geneva (independent), Lausanne  
**Source**: Bonjour et al. 1952 [MONOGRAPH]; neutrality established 1648.  
**ANACHRONISM FLAGS**: Geneva = independent 1763, joins Swiss Confederation 1815. Neuchâtel = Prussian until 1848.

### DENMARK-NORWAY
**Territorial extent**: Denmark proper (Jutland, Funen, Zealand, Bornholm); **Norway** (entire; subordinate since 1536); duchies of Schleswig & Holstein (personal union; Holstein = HRE status).  
**Capital**: Copenhagen  
**Key cities**: Bergen, Christiania (Oslo), Trondheim, Odense, Aarhus  
**Ruler**: Frederick V (r. 1746–66).  
**Source**: Derry 1979 [MONOGRAPH].  
**ANACHRONISM FLAGS**: **CRITICAL**: Norway = DANISH 1763, not independent. Union dissolves 1814 (Norway ceded to Sweden after Napoleonic Wars).

### SWEDEN
**Territorial extent**: Sweden proper; **Finland** (entire except Vyborg/Karelia ceded to Russia 1721); **Swedish Pomerania** (around Stralsund, north of Peene River + island of Rügen).  
**Capital**: Stockholm  
**Key cities**: Gothenburg, Uppsala, Turku (Åbo, Finland), Stralsund (Pomerania), Greifswald (Pomerania)  
**Ruler**: Adolf Frederick (Adolf Fredrik, r. 1751–71); **figurehead** during Age of Liberty (1719–72). Real power: Riksdag of the Estates (four estates: nobles, clergy, burghers, peasants). **Hats faction** (pro-French, mercantilist) controlled parliament 1763.  
**Seven Years' War outcome**: Treaty of Hamburg (22 May 1762) reaffirmed **status quo ante bellum** — Sweden retained Pomerania but failed to recover lost territories.  
**Source**: Roberts 1986 [MONOGRAPH] Age of Liberty; Derry 1979 [MONOGRAPH] Scandinavia; Wikipedia [WEAK] Swedish Pomerania extent.  
**ANACHRONISM FLAGS**:  
- **Finland**: Swedish 1763; lost to Russia 1809 (Treaty of Fredrikshamn). **By 1815 baseline, Finland = Russian Grand Duchy.**  
- **Vyborg/Karelia**: Russian since 1721 (Treaty of Nystad); NOT Swedish 1763.  
- **Swedish Pomerania**: Swedish 1763; ceded to Prussia 1815 (Congress of Vienna). **By 1815 baseline, Pomerania = Prussian.**

## POPULATION (anchors; mod scale 4000 people = 1 unit)

### France
**Total magnitude**: **~25 million** [WEAK/Wikipedia] (uncited in 1763_WORLD_WEurope.md; plausible range 24–26M based on period literature).  
**Composition**: Predominantly rural (80–85% subsistence agriculture per Doyle); urbanization concentrated in Paris (~600,000 [WEAK]), Lyon, Marseille, Bordeaux, Rouen.  
**Dominant culture(s) & religion(s)**: French-speaking Catholic majority; Huguenot Protestant minority (post-1685 diaspora; toleration not restored until 1787).  
**Source**: Doyle 1989 [MONOGRAPH] pre-Revolutionary demography; Wikipedia [WEAK] city populations.

### Great Britain (England, Wales, Scotland)
**Total magnitude**: **~7–8 million** [WEAK/Wikipedia] England+Wales+Scotland; England alone ~6M.  
**Regional density**: London dominant (~750,000 [WEAK]); Liverpool, Manchester, Birmingham growing industrial centers; Edinburgh, Glasgow (Scotland); rural Highlands depopulated.  
**Dominant culture(s) & religion(s)**: English Anglican majority (Church of England established); Scottish Presbyterian (Church of Scotland); Catholic minority (Ireland separate; see below).  
**Source**: Black 2001 [MONOGRAPH] 18th-century Britain; Wikipedia [WEAK] city populations.

### Ireland
**Total magnitude**: **~2–3 million** [WEAK/Wikipedia] (Ireland under British control post-1691).  
**Composition**: Majority Catholic Irish; Protestant Anglo-Irish Ascendancy ruling class; Ulster Scots-Irish (Presbyterian).  
**Dominant culture(s) & religion(s)**: Catholic Irish-speaking majority (Gaelic); Anglican Protestant Ascendancy (ruling elite); Penal Laws restrict Catholic rights.  
**Source**: Black 2001 [MONOGRAPH] Ireland governance; Wikipedia [WEAK] population estimate.

### Spain
**Total magnitude**: **~9 million** [WEAK/Wikipedia] (Spain proper; excludes American colonies).  
**Composition**: Predominantly rural Castilian core; Catalonia, Valencia, Basque Country regional identities; urbanization low except Madrid, Barcelona, Seville.  
**Dominant culture(s) & religion(s)**: Spanish-speaking Catholic (Inquisition still active; Jews/Muslims expelled/converted post-1492); regional languages (Catalan, Basque, Galician).  
**Source**: Lynch 1989 [MONOGRAPH] Bourbon Spain; Wikipedia [WEAK] population estimate.

### Portugal
**Total magnitude**: **~3 million** [WEAK/Wikipedia] (Portugal proper; excludes Brazil).  
**Composition**: Lisbon ~200,000 [WEAK] (rebuilt post-1755 earthquake); Porto; rural interior.  
**Dominant culture(s) & religion(s)**: Portuguese-speaking Catholic.  
**Source**: Maxwell 1995 [MONOGRAPH] Pombal era; Wikipedia [WEAK] population estimate.

### Dutch Republic
**Total magnitude**: **~2 million** [WEAK/Wikipedia] (seven provinces + Generality Lands).  
**Composition**: Amsterdam dominant (~200,000 [WEAK]); Rotterdam, Utrecht, Leiden, Haarlem; Holland province contributed >50% of tax quota (States of Holland → States General).  
**Dominant culture(s) & religion(s)**: Dutch-speaking Reformed Protestant majority (Calvinist); Catholic minority (~40% but politically subordinate); Sephardic Jewish community (Amsterdam).  
**Source**: Israel 1995 [MONOGRAPH] Dutch Republic decline; Wikipedia [WEAK] population estimate.

### Austrian Netherlands (Belgium)
**Total magnitude**: **~2–2.5 million** [WEAK/Wikipedia estimate] (modern Belgium + Luxembourg).  
**Composition**: Brussels, Antwerp, Ghent, Liège urban centers; Flemish rural majority.  
**Dominant culture(s) & religion(s)**: Flemish/Dutch-speaking north (Catholic); Walloon French-speaking south (Catholic).  
**Source**: Wikipedia [WEAK] Austrian Netherlands; no strong monograph data for 1763 population.

### Denmark-Norway
**Total magnitude**: **~1.5 million Denmark, ~0.75 million Norway** [PRIMARY] = **~2.25 million total** (based on 1763_econ_denmark_norway.md census-level inference; Denmark 80–85% rural).  
**Regional density**: Copenhagen dominant (~100,000 [PRIMARY/WEAK hybrid]); Bergen, Christiania (Oslo), Trondheim (Norway); Aarhus, Aalborg (Denmark).  
**Economic specialization**: Denmark = agricultural (subsistence+noble estates); Norway = industrial (timber, mining, fishing) with ~2/3 audited income transferred to Copenhagen [PRIMARY].  
**Dominant culture(s) & religion(s)**: Danish-speaking Lutheran (Denmark); Norwegian-speaking Lutheran (Norway).  
**Source**: 1763_econ_denmark_norway.md [PRIMARY/MONOGRAPH derivation]; Derry 1979 [MONOGRAPH] Scandinavia.

### Sweden (incl. Finland 1763)
**Total magnitude**: **~2 million Sweden proper, ~0.4 million Finland** [WEAK/Wikipedia estimate] = **~2.4 million total** (Sweden+Finland+Pomerania).  
**Regional density**: Stockholm (~70,000 [WEAK]); Gothenburg, Uppsala; Turku/Åbo (Finland); Stralsund (Pomerania ~20,000 [WEAK]).  
**Economic specialization**: Bergslagen region (Värmland, Västmanland, Dalarna, Gävleborg, Örebro) = iron/copper mining; Norrland forests (Västernorrland, Norrbotten) = timber/tar production [MONOGRAPH].  
**Dominant culture(s) & religion(s)**: Swedish-speaking Lutheran majority; Finnish-speaking Lutheran (Finland); Sami (northern Lapland).  
**Source**: Roberts 1986 [MONOGRAPH] Age of Liberty; 1763_econ_sweden_mining.md [MONOGRAPH/PRIMARY]; Wikipedia [WEAK] population estimates.

**Composition**: Bern, Zürich, Luzern, Basel urban centers; rural Alpine cantons.

### Denmark-Norway
**Norwegian primary sectors [PRIMARY]**:

1. **Timber and lumber (PRIMARY EXPORT)**: Major industry by 17th century; exports "especially to England" and foreign navies. Geographic centers: southern/eastern Norway river valleys (Glomma, Lægen); shipping ports Oslo (Christiania), Frederikstad, Moss, Sandefjord (east coast), Bergen (west coast). Royal decree 1688 restricted sawmill operations (anti-deforestation); small number of merchants controlled entire industry under Danish royal monopoly [PRIMARY].

2. **Fishing and preserved fish products**: **Stockfish** (dried unsalted cod) = "Norway's longest sustained export commodity" (13th century onward); ~80% water removed via cold-air fermentation, storage life several years; produced Feb–May during cod season; **northern Norway ideal** (Lofoten Islands). Shipped to Mediterranean (Italy as *stoccafisso*), Baltic, England. **Salted cod** (*klippfisk*) emerging 18th century; required salt purchased from Danish merchants (increased Danish control) [PRIMARY].

3. **Mining and metals**: **Kongsberg Silver Mines**, **Røros Copper Mines** (central Norway); operated under Danish royal monopoly charters [PRIMARY].

4. **Shipping and maritime trade**: Norwegian neutral-power advantage 1690–1763 (captured neutral shipping during European wars); supplied foreign navies; many Norwegians worked on foreign (Dutch) vessels. Recovery after Great Northern War (1709–21 disruption) [PRIMARY].

**Danish revenues [PRIMARY]**:  
- **Sound Dues** (Øresundstolden): **Constituted up to 2/3 of Denmark's state income 16th–17th centuries**; still significant 1763. Mechanics: All foreign ships crossing Øresund (Denmark-Sweden strait) halted at **Helsingør** (Elsinore); 1567 shift to **1–2% tax on cargo value** (tripled revenue); Crown right to purchase cargo at declared value (anti-underreporting enforcement); Danish cannons at Helsingør + Swedish cannons at Helsingborg. Traffic: 795 ships (1479) → 2,731 (1560) → 4,262 (1581); substantial by 18th century (all Baltic trade, Mediterranean grain, Northern European commerce) [PRIMARY].

- **Agricultural surplus**: Denmark = subsistence base (80–85% rural); supplied union; noble landowners dominated [PRIMARY].

- **Rents from Norwegian industry**: Up to 2/3 of Norway's audited national income transferred to Copenhagen (colonial-style revenue extraction) [PRIMARY].

- **Colonial trade**: Sugar/spices/slave trade (Tranquebar, West Indies) [PRIMARY].

**Key ports [PRIMARY]**:  
- Denmark: **Copenhagen** (capital, merchant hub, customs/Sound Dues HQ); **Helsingør** (toll collection); Aarhus, Aalborg.  
- Norway: **Oslo** (Christiania, timber export); **Frederikstad** (timber/shipping); Moss; **Bergen** (fish exports, North Sea/Atlantic hub); Stavanger (fishing); Trondheim; Lofoten Islands (cod production).

**Economic complementarity**: Denmark supplied food; Norway supplied timber/metals/fish/shipping. Norwegian industry enjoyed legal monopoly in Denmark. **Norway = industrial/export engine; Denmark = agricultural base + monopoly rent collector** [PRIMARY].

**Source**: 1763_econ_denmark_norway.md [PRIMARY] (census-level inference, trade mechanics); Derry 1979 [MONOGRAPH] Scandinavia.

### Sweden (incl. Finland 1763)
**Iron and copper mining (PRIMARY EXPORT) [MONOGRAPH/PRIMARY]**:

1. **Bergslagen iron mining region**: Central Sweden spanning Värmland, Västmanland, Dalarna, Gävleborg, Örebro provinces (~6,000–8,000 km²). **Production volumes**: ~42,000–46,000 tonnes/year bar-iron exports 1763 (interpolated; 1740s baseline ~40,000 tonnes, 1790s peak ~52,000 tonnes); English imports from Sweden ~20,000 tonnes/year. **Iron constituted ~75% of total Swedish exports by late 18th century** [PRIMARY].

   **Major districts**:  
   - **Värmland** (Filipstad, Sunne, Fryksdalen): Major bar-iron forges; critical export hub.  
   - **Västmanland** (Arboga, Västerås hinterland): Bloomery and forge operations.  
   - **Dalarna** (Falun, Hedemora, Säter): Mixed copper/iron (Kopparbergslagen); bar-iron forges.  
   - **Gävleborg** (Gävle, Ljusdal): Coastal trade exit; bar-iron shipment point.  
   - **Örebro** (Örebro, Lindesberg): Forges and re-export warehouses.

   **Production network**: Bloomeries (*småbruk*) using local bog ore + charcoal → Forges (*bruk*) converting pig iron to bar iron → Export pipeline via Gävle, Västerås, Värmland ports → Gothenburg (west coast) or Stockholm/Västervik (Baltic) → European markets (England, Netherlands, Hamburg) [PRIMARY].

2. **Falun copper mine** (Kopparberg, Dalarna province): Peak production era 17th century; still commercially active 1763 but secondary to 17th-century output. Refined copper bars for European markets; supplied domestic arms, bells, metalware. Part of Kopparbergslagen industrial district (copper+iron combined) [PRIMARY].

**Tar production (*pix suecica*) [PRIMARY]**: Primary source regions: Swedish Norrland boreal forests (Västernorrland, Jämtland, Norrbotten); Ume River valley, Torne River region; upper Dalarna, Gävleborg forest belt. Pine tar extraction via charring in pit kilns; dispersed cottage-industry (*tarverk*); seasonal (spring/early summer). Export: English/Dutch shipyards (naval stores); 5–10% of Swedish exports by value 1763 [PRIMARY].

**Timber/lumber exports [PRIMARY]**: Norrland forests (Västernorrland, Jämtland, Norrbotten) primary long-timber source; upper Svealand (Gävleborg, Dalarna, Västmanland) supplementary. Log-export routes: River drives (Göta Älv, Klarälv, Göte Älv) → west-coast ports. Buyers: English shipyards, Dutch traders, West European construction; 3–8% of export value [PRIMARY].

**Mining regulation**: **Bergskollegium** (Swedish Bureau of Mines, founded 1637): Crown monopoly over all mining; licensing of private forges; export licensing and tax collection; technical standardization (bar-iron quality, weight); travelogue documentation (study missions) [PRIMARY].

**Port infrastructure [PRIMARY]**:  
- **Gothenburg** (west coast): Primary export hub for Värmland iron and timber (Atlantic/North Sea routes).  
- **Gävle** (east coast, Gävleborg): Major iron and timber export point to Baltic.  
- **Stockholm** (central Baltic): Administrative hub; secondary export point for copper, tar, inland-origin goods.  
- **Västervik** (southeast, Östergötland): Secondary export point for tar and timber.

**Source**: 1763_econ_sweden_mining.md [MONOGRAPH/PRIMARY] (Jernkontoret 30-year Atlas project, Uppsala University research, Oslo copper studies, SCB historical statistics 1732–1970, Taylor & Francis 1982 peer-reviewed journal); Roberts 1986 [MONOGRAPH] Age of Liberty.

### Switzerland
