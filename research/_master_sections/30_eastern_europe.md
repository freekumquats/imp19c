## OWNERSHIP (political control, Feb 1763)

### Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth (Crown of Poland + Grand Duchy of Lithuania, royal union)

**FULL pre-partition extent** — the Commonwealth stretched across approximately 830,000 km², making it one of Europe's largest states in 1763:

#### Crown Lands (Kingdom of Poland)
- **Greater Poland (Wielkopolska)** — Capital: Poznań. Primary grain-producing region; wheat and rye exports via Vistula/Oder river access to Danzig. West-central location; direct Oder River competitor to Vistula route. [1763_econ_poland_lithuania.md lines 32-36]
- **Lesser Poland (Małopolska)** — Capital: Kraków (coronation city). South-central; includes Galicia (Red Ruthenia, capital Lwów/Lviv). Rye and barley production; smaller szlachta landholdings, more fragmented than Greater Poland. Vistula River access via Kraków collection point. [lines 38-42, 111]
- **Mazovia (Masovia)** — Capital: Warsaw (administrative capital of Commonwealth since 1596). Central Poland north of Vistula; the **most productive grain region** in the Commonwealth. Massive enserfed populations (50-100k serfs per county) on szlachta estates; rye/wheat/barley exports. Warsaw is major Vistula warehouse/transit hub, 300 km from Danzig. [lines 18-24, 113]
- **Podlachia** — Northeast of Mazovia; forms transitional zone between Poland and Lithuania. Grain and forest products. [inferred from 1763_rulers_poland_lithuania.md territorial list]
- **Royal Prussia (Pomerelia)** — Capital: Danzig/Gdańsk (free city under Polish king). Baltic coast; includes Toruń and Elbląg. **Primary export terminus** for Commonwealth grain; 50,000-80,000 tons/year passed through Danzig to Dutch, English, French, Spanish markets. Vistula River mouth; deep-water harbor. [1763_econ_poland_lithuania.md lines 123-143]
- **Galicia / Red Ruthenia** — Capital: Lwów (Lviv). South-central Commonwealth (modern Ukrainian border); Carpathian foothills. Included in Lesser Poland administrative structure. [line 39]
- **Volhynia** — West-central Ukraine; RIGHT-bank (west of Dnieper). Part of Lesser Poland Province. Grain production region. [1763_rulers_poland_lithuania.md line 11]
- **Podolia** — Capital: Kamianets-Podilskyi. South-central Commonwealth (modern Ukrainian border). **Magnate-dominated** (Potocki, Czartoryski families); estates often 50k+ hectares. Rye (primary), barley, oats. Dniester River link to Ottoman Black Sea trade (secondary to Vistula). [1763_econ_poland_lithuania.md lines 26-30]
- **RIGHT-BANK UKRAINE** — West of Dnieper River: Bila Tserkva, Fastiv, Cherkasy, Berdychiv. Part of Lesser Poland Province (Kiev and Bracław voivodeships). [1763_rulers_poland_lithuania.md line 11; audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 58]

#### Grand Duchy of Lithuania (federated state, separate administration/army/treasury/courts/seal)
- **Lithuania proper** — Capital: **Vilnius** (Grand Duchy seat). Core lands including Trakai voivodeship. Rye and barley production; lower average productivity than Poland proper. High enserfment rates (barshchina obligation 3-4 days/week standard). Neman River export route to Memel/Baltic; competed with Danzig merchants. [1763_econ_poland_lithuania.md lines 44-49; 1763_rulers_poland_lithuania.md lines 19-22]
- **Samogitia (Žemaitija)** — Northwestern Lithuania; eldership status. Baltic coastal access. Grain and timber. [1763_econ_poland_lithuania.md line 172; 1763_rulers_poland_lithuania.md line 20]
- **Belarus** — Centers: Minsk, Polotsk, Vitebsk. Eastern Grand Duchy territories. Grain, timber, flax/hemp production. [1763_rulers_poland_lithuania.md line 21; audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 58]
- **Latgalia** — Eastern Latvia; cities Daugavpils (Dünaburg) and Rēzekne. Part of Grand Duchy since medieval period. [audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 58]
- **Livonia (Duchy of Livonia / Inflanty)** — Southern Baltic; part of Grand Duchy administration. [1763_rulers_poland_lithuania.md line 22]
- **Smolensk** — **CORRECTION**: Smolensk was ceded to Russia by the Treaty of Andrusovo (1667) and confirmed in the Eternal Peace Treaty of 1686. NOT Commonwealth in 1763. [audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 58 lists it as Commonwealth, but line 44 under Russia contradicts this; cross-reference to 1667 treaty resolves in favor of Russian ownership]

**Territorial span**: Approximately 830,000 km² in Feb 1763. Stretched from Baltic Sea to Black Sea region, covering present-day Poland, Lithuania, Belarus, most of Latvia, and much of Ukraine west of the Dnieper. [1763_rulers_poland_lithuania.md line 33]

**Political structure**: Elective monarchy (Augustus III, Wettin dynasty, r. 1733-1763; died 5 Oct 1763). Szlachta (nobility) 8-15% of population with political rights (elected monarch, served in Sejm bicameral parliament, held land allodially). "Golden Liberty" system. Weak central authority; Sejm gridlocked by *liberum veto* (any one noble could block legislation). Federation of Crown + Grand Duchy; Warsaw administrative capital; Kraków coronation city. [1763_rulers_poland_lithuania.md lines 36-65; 1763_econ_poland_lithuania.md lines 265-275]

**Sources**: Kaminsky (1993), Frost (2000), Stone (2001), Kula (1976), Rutkowski (1947), Lukowski & Zawadzki (2006), Hamish M. Scott (2001)

### Russia (Romanov Empire, Catherine II acceded 1762)

**Extent in Feb 1763**:

#### European Russia
- **Core territories**: European Russia west to the Urals; includes Central Black Earth Belt (Voronezh, Kursk, Tambov oblasts — primary wheat/rye/barley), Volga Basin (Nizhny Novgorod, Kazan — rye/barley; Volga River transport corridor), Western Russia/Smolensk-Moscow corridor (rye/barley/oats), Tver, Yaroslavl, Pskov, Novgorod (hemp/flax production zones). [1763_econ_russia_baltic.md lines 9-28, 240-262]

#### Baltic Provinces (acquired 1721, Treaty of Nystad)
- **Livonia** — Capital: Riga. Daugava River access; major timber export hub (10,000+ lasts annually; 1 last ≈ 2 tons); flax, hemp, grain (minor). Russian-ruled since 1710 capture, formalized 1721. Hanseatic guild merchant structure retained (negotiated charter). [1763_econ_russia_baltic.md lines 68-84]
- **Estonia** — Capital: Reval/Tallinn. Gulf of Finland coast; medieval port. Flax, hemp, timber, modest grain. Smaller volume than Riga or St. Petersburg; feeder port for regional production. [lines 85-92]
- **Ingria** — Region around St. Petersburg. **St. Petersburg** founded 1703; primary grain/hemp/flax/timber export hub. 200,000-400,000 quarters grain annually (1750s-1760s); tens of thousands of tons hemp/flax. Connected via Volga River system to central/southern Russia. [lines 53-66, 268]
- **Vyborg / Karelia** — Southeastern Finland; ceded by Sweden in Treaty of Nystad (1721). Tar/pitch production (northern forests; spruce/pine distillation); timber. [audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 75 correction; 1763_econ_russia_furs_urals.md lines 143-148]

#### LEFT-BANK UKRAINE + KIEV (acquired 1667, Treaty of Andrusovo)
- **LEFT-BANK UKRAINE** — East of Dnieper River. Includes Kiev city (west bank, but ceded to Russia; right-bank territories Polish). Grain surplus (wheat, barley); flows northward to Russian wholesalers or westward to Poland/Baltic. Hetmanate structure (Cossack autonomy) until 1764 abolition. [1763_econ_russia_baltic.md lines 26-28; audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 44]
- **SLOBODA UKRAINE** — East of Dnieper; Kharkiv region. Russian settlement zone; grain production. [audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 44]
- **ZAPORIZHIAN SICH** — Cossack autonomous region; Dnieper rapids. Under Russian suzerainty (abolished 1775). [audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 44]

#### Smolensk
- **Smolensk** — Western Russia; ceded by Poland-Lithuania in Treaty of Andrusovo (1667). Rye, hemp, flax production. Western Dvina/Daugava River route connects to Riga. [1763_econ_russia_baltic.md line 252; audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 44]

#### Urals and Siberia (resource extraction zones)
- **Nevyansk** — Core Demidov ironworks hub; Neyva River (Ob basin tributary), 97 km north of Yekaterinburg. Founded 1700; 3.9M acres land grant. Iron foundry, smeltery, gold mine. Old Believers settlement. [1763_econ_russia_furs_urals.md lines 73-82]
- **Yekaterinburg** — Founded 1723; Iset River, eastern slope Urals. **Largest iron factory in world at construction**; state/Demidov blast furnaces. Verkh-Isetsky plant (1726), Uktus/Shuvakish ironworks. By 1763: dominant Urals production center. [lines 83-93]
- **Perm** — Mulyanka River, west-central Urals. Founded ~1723-1726; copper smelter (Georg Wilhelm de Gennin). [lines 95-99]
- **Tobolsk** — Ob River, Western Siberia. Fur trade hub; customs center for yasak (tribute) collection from indigenous Siberians. [lines 36-37]
- **Yakutsk** — Lena River, far-eastern Siberia. Far-eastern fur collection hub. [line 39]
- **Yeniseysk** — Yenisei River, central Siberia. Fur collection point. [line 38]
- **Arkhangelsk** — White Sea, Arctic coast. Tar/pitch/timber export hub (naval stores; Russian state monopoly). Historic trade route (pre-St. Petersburg); declining share post-1703 but still significant (~30,000-50,000 tons annually by 1760s). [1763_econ_russia_baltic.md lines 94-104; 1763_econ_russia_furs_urals.md lines 141-176]

#### NOT Russian in 1763
- **CRIMEA** — Independent Crimean Khanate (Giray dynasty), Ottoman vassal. Annexed by Russia in 1783, NOT before. [audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md lines 42, 45-46, 362]
- **RIGHT-BANK UKRAINE** — West of Dnieper; Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth territory. NOT Russian until Second Partition (1793). [audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 58]
- **PARTITION LANDS** — Any Polish territories (Galicia, Greater Poland, Lithuania, Belarus west of 1772 line, Latvia, Courland) are NOT Russian in 1763. [audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 361]
- **FINLAND** (except Vyborg/Karelia) — Swedish until 1809. NOT Russian in 1763. [audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 366]

**Capital**: St. Petersburg (since 1712). Secondary: Moscow. [audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 43]

**Ruler**: Catherine II (acceded 1762 after Peter III's brief reign). [1763_econ_russia_baltic.md lines 180-196]

**Sources**: Bater (1976), Madariaga (1981), Anderson (1954), Davies (1997), Vodarski (1977), Kulisher (1923), Mironov (1991), Dixon (2009), Fisher (1978)

### Courland (Duchy of Courland and Semigallia)

**Status in 1763**: Vassal duchy; fief of the Polish Crown. Under Russian influence but NOT Russian sovereign territory. [1763_rulers_poland_lithuania.md lines 26-27; audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 27]

**Ruler**: Ernst Johann von Biron (reappointed by Catherine II of Russia in 1763 after earlier exile; ruled 1763-1769). [1763_rulers_poland_lithuania.md line 27]

**Location**: Baltic coast; modern Latvia (western regions: Jelgava/Mitau, Liepāja/Libau).

**ANACHRONISM FLAG**: Courland is a Polish fief in 1763, NOT a Russian possession. It falls fully under Russian control only after the Third Partition (1795).

### Crimean Khanate

**Status in 1763**: **Independent state**, vassal of the Ottoman Empire. Giray dynasty ruling house. [audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md lines 42, 45-46, 55]

**Capital**: Bakhchisaray.

**Extent**: Crimean Peninsula; steppe territories north of Crimea (Nogai pastoralists).

**Annexed by Russia**: 1783, NOT before. [audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 362]

**ANACHRONISM FLAG**: Crimea MUST be independent (Crimean Khanate, Ottoman vassal) in 1763. Any representation as Russian territory before 1783 is anachronistic. [audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md lines 42, 46-47, 362]

**Source**: Fisher (1978), Finkel (2005)

## POPULATION (anchors; mod scale 4000 people = 1 unit)

### Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth (aggregate ~11-12 million)

**Aggregate figure**: 11-12 million total population in 1763. [Inferred from 1763_econ_poland_lithuania.md serf population percentages and regional descriptions; explicit aggregate not stated, but consistent with historiographic consensus for pre-partition Commonwealth — Stone (2001), Lukowski & Zawadzki (2006)]

#### Rural / Serf Composition
- **Enserfment rate**: 60-75% of rural population in core grain regions (Mazovia, Greater Poland, Podolia, Lesser Poland). [1763_econ_poland_lithuania.md line 94]
- **Serf categories**:
  - *Kmiecie* (peasant-farmers): Hereditary semi-free status; held allotments; owed barshchina (labor obligation) 3-6 days/week on demesne.
  - *Parobcy* (farmhands): Landless; owed unlimited labor; extreme servility.
  - *Dzierżawcy* (leaseholders): Middle category; sometimes free; paid cash rent. [lines 96-99]
- **Labor intensity**: Grain monoculture required peak labor 6-8 weeks (May-Sept harvest); folwarki (demesne estates) supplemented serf labor with hired workers (often Jewish) during harvest. [lines 101-102]

#### Magnate Landownership
- **Magnate class**: 20-40 richest families owned ~15-20% of Commonwealth land and ~40-50% of cultivable demesne. [line 161]
- **Enserfed population under magnates**: 3,000-15,000 serfs per magnate family. Example: Potocki family (Podolia/Ruthenia) controlled 8,000-12,000 tons/year grain output from latifundia >200k hectares (~400k hectares across holdings). [lines 165-179]

#### Szlachta (Nobility)
- **Proportion**: 8-15% of total population (unusually high compared to ~1% in France/Italy). [1763_rulers_poland_lithuania.md line 45]
- **Legal status**: Hereditary nobility; held land allodially; elected monarch; served in Sejm; "Golden Liberty" protections. [lines 46-54]

#### Urban / Merchant
- **Danzig/Gdańsk**: Free city; deep-water harbor. Grain merchant community: Dutch (40-45% of trade), English (15-20%), Hanseatic Germans, Danzig burghers (Polish merchants), Jewish traders (license-holders as middlemen). [1763_econ_poland_lithuania.md lines 130-136]
- **Warsaw**: Administrative capital; wholesale/transit hub for Vistula grain traffic. Magnates maintained urban residences; burgher/Jewish merchant agents. [lines 113, 178]

#### Ethnic / Religious Composition
- **Polish** (Catholic): Dominant in Crown lands (Greater Poland, Mazovia, Lesser Poland).
- **Lithuanian** (Catholic): Dominant in Grand Duchy core (Vilnius, Trakai, Samogitia).
- **Ruthenian / Ukrainian** (Orthodox, Uniate): Right-bank Ukraine (Volhynia, Podolia, Kiev voivodeship); Belarus (Minsk, Polotsk, Vitebsk). Uniate Church (1596 Union of Brest) significant in western Ukraine and Belarus.
- **Jewish**: Urban centers (Warsaw, Kraków, Vilnius, Lwów); merchant/leaseholder/agent roles; rural areas (manor agents, distillery/mill operators, harvest labor). Estimated ~750,000-1 million Jews in Commonwealth by 1764 (largest Jewish population in world). [Inferred from 1763_econ_poland_lithuania.md merchant roles; historiographic consensus — Stone (2001)]
- **German**: Royal Prussia urban centers (Danzig, Toruń, Elbląg); burgher class. Baltic Livonia towns.
- **Latvian / Latgalian**: Latgalia (Daugavpils, Rēzekne region); Grand Duchy territories.

**Sources**: Stone (2001), Kula (1976), Rutkowski (1947), Lukowski & Zawadzki (2006) [MONOGRAPH grade for aggregate; WEAK grade for ethnic proportions due to 18th-century census gaps]

### Russia (European Russia ~20 million; Siberia sparse)

**Aggregate figure**: European Russia (west of Urals) ~20 million in 1763. [1763_econ_russia_baltic.md — Vodarski (1977) cited; Mironov (1991) regional production/tax data imply demographic base]

#### Regional Breakdowns (estimates; mod scale 4000:1 → divide by 4000 for unit count)

**Central Black Earth Belt** (Voronezh, Kursk, Tambov):
- Most productive grain region; wheat/rye/barley.
- Serf-based agricultural labor.
- Estimated ~3-4 million population. [Inferred from 1763_econ_russia_baltic.md lines 11-15; Vodarski (1977) demographic data]

**Volga Basin** (Nizhny Novgorod, Kazan):
- Secondary grain producer; rye/barley.
- Mixed Russian/Tatar/Finno-Ugric population.
- Estimated ~2-3 million. [lines 17-20]

**Moscow-Tula-Ryazan corridor**:
- Hemp/flax/linseed oil; local grain consumption.
- Urban center (Moscow); processing mills.
- Estimated ~2-3 million (including Moscow city ~300k). [lines 22-24; 1763_econ_russia_furs_urals.md lines 159-161]

**Northwestern provinces** (Pskov, Novgorod, Tver, Yaroslavl, Smolensk):
- Hemp/flax production zones (primary); rye/barley.
- Estimated ~2-3 million. [1763_econ_russia_baltic.md lines 32-45, 254-259]

**Baltic provinces** (Livonia/Riga region, Estonia/Reval, Ingria/St. Petersburg):
- **St. Petersburg city**: ~150,000-200,000 (imperial capital, founded 1703). [Bater (1976)]
- **Riga**: ~30,000-40,000 (major Baltic port, Hanseatic legacy). [Inferred from trade volume]
- **Reval/Tallinn**: ~10,000-15,000 (secondary port).
- **Rural Livonia/Estonia**: Mixed Latvian/Estonian peasantry (serf/corvée labor), Baltic German landowning nobility, Russian administration. Estimated ~500,000-700,000 total Baltic provinces. [Madariaga (1981); Davies (1997)]

**Left-bank Ukraine + Sloboda + Zaporizhia**:
- Ukrainian/Cossack population; grain surplus region.
- **Kiev city**: ~20,000-30,000 (religious/administrative center).
- Estimated ~2-3 million left-bank territories. [Inferred from 1763_econ_russia_baltic.md lines 26-28; historiographic consensus]

**Urals industrial zone** (Nevyansk, Yekaterinburg, Perm):
- **Nevyansk**: 50-200 serfs per folwark average; Demidov estates 3,000-15,000 serfs. Old Believers settlement. [1763_econ_russia_furs_urals.md line 79]
- **Yekaterinburg**: Largest iron factory; garrison town; estimated ~5,000-10,000. [line 89]
- **Perm**: Copper smelter; smaller; ~3,000-5,000. [line 99]
- Estimated ~100,000-200,000 Urals industrial belt total.

**Siberia** (Tobolsk, Yakutsk, Yeniseysk, Arkhangelsk):
- **Sparse population**: Indigenous yasak tribute-payers (Tungus, Buryats, Siberians); Russian fur traders/administrators; Old Believers/exile communities.
- **Tobolsk**: ~10,000-15,000 (fur trade hub). [line 36]
- **Yakutsk**: ~3,000-5,000 (far-eastern outpost). [line 39]
- **Arkhangelsk**: ~10,000-15,000 (Arctic port; naval stores). [line 174]
- Estimated ~300,000-500,000 Siberia total (vast territory, low density). [Bobrick (1992), Fisher (1943) cited in 1763_econ_russia_furs_urals.md]

#### Serf Population
- **Enserfment rate**: ~50-60% of rural population in European Russia (lower than Poland-Lithuania's 60-75%, but higher absolute numbers). [Melton (1998) cited in 1763_econ_russia_baltic.md line 304]
- **Barshchina obligation**: 3-4 days/week standard (similar to Poland-Lithuania). [1763_econ_poland_lithuania.md line 48 — parallel system]

#### Ethnic / Religious Composition
- **Russian** (Orthodox): Dominant in European Russia, Siberia (settlers). Estimated ~70-75% of population.
- **Ukrainian** (Orthodox, some Uniate): Left-bank Ukraine, Sloboda. Estimated ~10-12%.
- **Tatar** (Muslim): Volga Basin (Kazan Khanate successor population), Crimea (independent). Estimated ~3-5% European Russia.
- **Finno-Ugric** (animist/Orthodox): Northern forests (Karelia), Volga Basin. Estimated ~3-4%.
- **Baltic Germans**: Livonia/Estonia landowning nobility; Lutheran. Estimated <1% (but politically significant).
- **Latvian / Estonian**: Baltic provinces peasantry; Lutheran/Orthodox. Estimated ~2-3%.
- **Polish / Lithuanian**: Smolensk region (border zones); Catholic/Uniate. Estimated ~1-2%.
- **Jewish**: Urban centers (Kiev, Smolensk, Riga); restricted settlement zones. Estimated <1% European Russia (much smaller than Poland-Lithuania).
- **Indigenous Siberians**: Tungus, Buryats, Yakuts, etc.; yasak tribute-payers. Estimated ~300,000-400,000 Siberia. [1763_econ_russia_furs_urals.md lines 21-26]

**Sources**: Vodarski (1977), Mironov (1991), Bater (1976), Madariaga (1981), Melton (1998), Davies (1997), Bobrick (1992), Fisher (1943) [MONOGRAPH grade for aggregate ~20M European Russia; WEAK grade for regional/ethnic breakdowns due to 18th-century census limitations]

## KEY ANACHRONISM CHECKLIST (Eastern Europe)

### OWNERSHIP ANACHRONISMS (1763 vs. later eras)

**✅ Commonwealth WHOLE (no partitions till 1772/93/95)**
- **First Partition**: 1772 (Russia takes eastern Belarus, Latgalia; Prussia takes Royal Prussia west of Vistula, Ermland; Austria takes Galicia/Red Ruthenia). [audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 361]
- **Second Partition**: 1793 (Russia takes right-bank Ukraine, central Belarus; Prussia takes Greater Poland/Poznań, Danzig). [line 361]
- **Third Partition**: 1795 (Commonwealth erased; Russia takes Lithuania, Courland, rest of Belarus; Prussia takes Mazovia/Warsaw; Austria takes Kraków region). [line 361]

### TERRITORIAL EXTENT ANACHRONISMS (border precision)

**Greater Poland (Wielkopolska) extent**: Poznań capital; Vistula/Oder river access. Western boundary with Holy Roman Empire (Brandenburg-Prussia) stable since 1660s (Treaty of Oliva). Eastern boundary with Mazovia/Lesser Poland internal Commonwealth division. [1763_econ_poland_lithuania.md lines 32-36]

**Lesser Poland (Małopolska) extent**: Kraków capital; includes Galicia/Red Ruthenia (Lwów). Southern boundary with Habsburg Hungary (Carpathian crest). Eastern boundary with Volhynia/Podolia (internal divisions). [lines 38-42]

**Mazovia extent**: Warsaw capital; north of Vistula River. Western boundary with Greater Poland/Royal Prussia. Eastern boundary with Podlachia/Lithuania. [lines 18-24]

**Royal Prussia extent**: Danzig/Gdańsk, Toruń, Elbląg on Baltic coast. Western boundary with Brandenburg-Prussia (post-1660 Treaty of Oliva: Prussia holds Ducal Prussia/Königsberg, Poland holds Royal Prussia/Pomerelia). [lines 123-143; audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 58]

**Podolia extent**: South-central Commonwealth; Dniester River forms southern boundary with Ottoman Empire (Moldavia/Wallachia). Eastern boundary with right-bank Ukraine (internal division). [1763_econ_poland_lithuania.md lines 26-30]

**Lithuania proper extent**: Vilnius capital; includes Trakai, Samogitia. Western boundary with Ducal Prussia (Brandenburg-Prussia). Northern boundary with Livonia (Russian since 1721; formerly Swedish/Polish contested). Southern boundary with Belarus (internal Grand Duchy division). [lines 44-49; 1763_rulers_poland_lithuania.md lines 19-22]

**Belarus extent**: Minsk, Polotsk, Vitebsk. Eastern boundary with Russia (post-1667 Treaty of Andrusovo: Russia holds Smolensk, Poland holds Polotsk/Vitebsk/Minsk). [audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 58]

**Latgalia extent**: Daugavpils (Dünaburg), Rēzekne. Northern boundary with Russian Livonia (Riga region; post-1721). Eastern boundary with Russia (Pskov region). [audit_worklists/research/europe_1763.md line 58]

### POPULATION ANACHRONISMS (demographic change over time)

**Serf emancipation**: Serfdom remains dominant in both Poland-Lithuania (60-75% enserfment) and Russia (~50-60%) in 1763. Polish serfdom abolished 1864 (after partitions, by occupying powers). Russian serfdom abolished 1861 (Emancipation Reform). Do NOT apply post-emancipation demographics to 1763. [1763_econ_poland_lithuania.md line 94; Melton (1998) cited in 1763_econ_russia_baltic.md]

**Jewish population growth**: Commonwealth Jewish population ~750k-1M in 1763 (largest in world). Russian Empire Jewish population <1% European Russia (Pale of Settlement restrictions; Jews concentrated in former Polish territories post-partitions). Do NOT apply post-partition Jewish demographics (Russian Empire absorbing Polish Jews) to 1763 Russia. [Inferred from 1763_econ_poland_lithuania.md merchant roles; Stone (2001)]

**Urban growth**: St. Petersburg ~150k-200k in 1763 (rapid growth from 1703 founding). Moscow ~300k. Warsaw ~30k-40k. Riga ~30k-40k. Do NOT apply 19th-century industrial-era urban populations. [Bater (1976); Davies (1997)]

**Siberian colonization**: Siberia remains sparsely populated (~300k-500k total) in 1763. Major Russian settlement expansion post-1800s (Trans-Siberian Railway era). Do NOT apply 19th/20th-century Siberian demographics. [Bobrick (1992), Fisher (1943) cited in 1763_econ_russia_furs_urals.md]
