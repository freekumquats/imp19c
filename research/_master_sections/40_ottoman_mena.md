### Ottoman autonomous vassals / tributaries

#### Crimean Khanate

**Khan**: Qırım Giray (قرم كراى, first reign 1758–1764; in power throughout 1763)  
**Dynasty**: Giray dynasty (exclusively appointed by Sultan from this family after 1584)  
**Capital**: Bakhchisaray  
**Status**: **Privileged vassal** (Kolodziejczyk: nuanced between vassalage and independence)

**Unusual vassal model**:
- **NO regular tribute paid to Ottomans** (reverse flow: Ottomans paid subsidies and gifts to Khanate)
- **Military service exchange**: Primary obligation was providing tens of thousands of light cavalry for Ottoman campaigns (considered "indispensable" / *vazgeçilemez*)
- **High internal autonomy**: Full control over domestic administration, justice, taxation, religious affairs (Khan held title "Caliph of the North")
- **Constrained foreign policy**: Especially with Russia required Ottoman coordination; Ottoman military presence in coastal fortresses (e.g., Caffa/Kefe)
- **Appointment**: Sultan had full appointment and removal authority by 1763, selecting exclusively from Giray dynasty

**CRITICAL DELTA**: Crimean Khanate **EXISTS in 1763**, **ABSENT in 1815** (annexed by Russia 1783)

Source: [MONOGRAPH] research/1763_rulers_crimea_ottoman_vassals.md lines 9-90; Kolodziejczyk (2011) *The Crimean Khanate and Poland-Lithuania*; Williams (2013) *The Sultan's Raiders*; Fisher (1978) *The Crimean Tatars*

#### Danubian Principalities (Moldavia & Wallachia)

**Phanariote System** (inaugurated 1711 Moldavia, 1715 Wallachia): After local elective systems abandoned, Ottomans appointed **Phanariote Greeks** from Constantinople through bribery-based system.

**Moldavia**:
- **Hospodar**: Grigore Callimachi (Γρηγόριος Καλλιμάχης, first reign 1761–1764; in power throughout 1763)
- **Status**: Ottoman vassal with high internal autonomy
- **Appointment**: Candidates typically served as chief dragoman of the Porte, purchased office via generous bribe
- **Tribute**: Paid directly to Porte; levels "directly proportional with Ottoman requests" (declined significantly after 1774 Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca to ~3,100 gold coins by 1820s)
- **Autonomy**: Retained internal self-government (own princes, courts, boyar councils), full control over domestic administration/justice, autonomous in religious affairs (Orthodox Christian)
- **Constraints**: Princely appointments entirely dependent on Sultan; no independent foreign policy; required to participate in Ottoman military campaigns when requested; **Russian intervention rights** established after 1774 Treaty

**Wallachia**:
- **Hospodar**: Constantin Mavrocordat (Κωνσταντίνος Μαυροκορδάτος, reign 1761–1763; in power during February 1763, reign ending that year)
- **Status**: Ottoman vassal with high internal autonomy
- **Appointment**: Same bribery-based system as Moldavia; **Wallachia commanded higher bribes** than Moldavia (wealthier province)
- **Tribute**: Paid to Porte; dropped from ~50,000 to ~20,000 gold coins by 1820s after 1774 Treaty
- **Autonomy**: Same high degree of internal autonomy as Moldavia
- **Constraints**: Same as Moldavia

**1763 vs 1815 Delta**: Vassal status continues both dates, but **Russian influence increases** after 1774 Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca, creating dual Ottoman-Russian pressure by 1815.

Source: [MONOGRAPH] research/1763_rulers_crimea_ottoman_vassals.md lines 92-289; Berza (1957) "Haraciul Moldovei și al Țării Românești"; Djuvara (1995) *Între Orient și Occident*; Glenny (2001) *The Balkans*

### Independent MENA states (NOT Ottoman)

#### Morocco (Alaouite Sultanate)

**Sultan**: Mohammed III / Sidi Muhammad ibn Abdallah (محمد بن عبد الله, r. 1757–1790)  
**Dynasty**: Alaouite (الدولة العلوية)  
**Capitals**: Marrakesh (imperial capital), Fez (traditional capital)  
**Status**: **Fully independent** — "the only North African nation to escape Ottoman dominion"

**Territorial extent**: Morocco (Atlantic + Mediterranean coasts, Atlas Mountains, northern Sahara oases); NOT under Ottoman control

**Key developments (around 1763)**:
- 1760–1764: Founded Essaouira (الصويرة / Mogador) fortified port city; completed by 1769–1770
- 1767: Closed Agadir's harbor, redirecting European trade toward Essaouira
- Governance: Decentralized, lighter taxation, relying on European trade revenue

**Economy**:
- European trade: Atlantic ports (Essaouira, Tangier, Salé, Rabat)
- Grain exports (wheat, barley), leather goods, limited sugar production
- Trans-Saharan trade (gold, enslaved people, salt caravans from Timbuktu/Sahel)
- Corsairing (Salé corsairs, declining by mid-18th century)

**1763 vs 1815 Delta**: Morocco fully independent under Alaouite dynasty **both dates** (1763: Mohammed III; 1815: Sulayman ibn Mohammed)

#### Oman (Al Bu Said Dynasty)

**Ruler**: Ahmad bin Said Al Bu Said (أحمد بن سعيد البوسعيدي, r. 1749–1783; elected Imam 9 June 1749)  
**Capital**: Rustaq  
**Status**: **Fully independent**; founder of the Al Bu Said dynasty (الأسرة البوسعيدية)

**Territorial extent**:
- **Oman**: Muscat, Rustaq, interior Oman
- **East African coast**: Zanzibar, Pemba, Mafia, Mombasa (Omani control established earlier under Yaruba dynasty; Fort Jesus at Mombasa fell to Omanis 1698)

**Military and economic strength**:
- Built Oman's **first permanent standing army and navy**
- Controlled "trade movement between Africa and Asia"
- Maritime empire dominating western Indian Ocean

**Economy**:
- **Muscat**: Major port for Indian Ocean trade (spices, textiles, coffee, dates)
- **Dates**: Major export from interior oases
- **Frankincense and myrrh**: From Dhofar region
- **Maritime trade**: Controlling sea routes between East Africa, Arabia, Persia, and India
- **East African goods**: Ivory, enslaved people, cloves (later, 19th century)

**1763 vs 1815 Delta**: Oman independent under Al Bu Said dynasty **both dates** (1763: Ahmad bin Said; 1815: Said bin Sultan, who would move capital to Zanzibar 1832)

#### Yemen (Qasimid State / Zaidi Imamate)

**Ruler**: Imam Al-Mahdi Abbas (الإمام المهدي عباس, r. 1748–1775)  
**Status**: **Independent** Zaidi imamate (Qasimid State / الدولة القاسمية)  
**Capital**: Sana'a (صنعاء)

**Territorial extent**:
- **Highland Yemen**: Zaidi-controlled northern highlands
- **Lost territory**: Sultanate of Lahej (سلطنة لحج) broke away and became "completely independent" in 1740
- **Fragmentation**: "The imamate did not follow a cohesive succession mechanism, and family feuds and tribal insubordination led to political decline in the 18th century"

**Economy**:
- **Coffee**: Mocha (المخا) coffee trade — Yemen's famous export to Europe
  - **Economic decline**: Europeans broke Yemen's coffee monopoly in first half of 18th century by smuggling coffee plants and cultivating them in colonies (Java, Caribbean, Brazil)
  - By 1763, Yemeni coffee still significant but facing competition
- **Agriculture**: Highland terraced farming (coffee, qat, grains)
- **Red Sea trade**: Mocha port connecting to Egypt, Hejaz, India

**1763 vs 1815 Delta**: Yemen independent under Zaidi imams **both dates**, but politically fractured; increasing fragmentation between 1763 and 1815; tribal autonomy grew stronger; would not be reincorporated into Ottoman Empire until 1849

Source: [MONOGRAPH] 1763_WORLD_Ottoman_MENA.md lines 674-714; [PRIMARY] asia_middleeast_1763.md line 17; Wikipedia: Qasimid State; Arabic Wikipedia: الدولة القاسمية

#### First Saudi State (Emirate of Diriyah)

**Ruler**: Muhammad bin Saud (محمد بن سعود, r. 1744–1765; founder)  
**Capital**: Diriyah (الدرعية) in Najd  
**Status**: **Independent emirate** in Najd (central Arabian plateau)  
**Ideology**: Wahhabi movement (الدعوة الوهابية) — founded 1744 through Diriyah Pact (ميثاق الدرعية) with Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab

**Territory (1763)**: Najd only; did **NOT yet control Hejaz** (Mecca/Medina remained under Ottoman-appointed Sharifs)

**Status in 1763**: The First Saudi State existed and was expanding, but still faced serious threats. In October 1764 (one year after game start), the Ismāʿīlī Shīʿa of Najrān allied with the 'Ujman tribe and defeated the Saudis at the Battle of Hair, "killing around 500 men" and besieging Diriyya (though the siege failed).

**Major opponent**: Dahham bin Dawas (دهام بن دواس), emir of Riyadh, fought the Saudis in "17 battles over 27 years"

**CRITICAL DELTA FOR 1763 vs 1815 BOOKMARK**:
- **1763**: First Saudi State **EXISTS** as independent emirate in Najd
- **1815**: First Saudi State **DOES NOT EXIST** — destroyed by Ottoman-Egyptian forces 1811–1818
  - Last ruler Abdullah bin Saud executed in Istanbul December 1818
  - Diriyah demolished by Ibrahim Pasha
  - Second Saudi State would not emerge until 1824

**This is the single most important political difference between 1763 and 1815 Arabia.**

#### Persia (Zand Dynasty)

**Ruler**: Karim Khan Zand (کریم خان زند, c. 1705–1779)  
**Title**: *Vakil ol-Ra'aya* (وکیل الرعایا, "Deputy of the People" / "Representative of the People") — **notably refused** the title "Shah," adopting a humbler designation  
**Capital**: **Shiraz** (شیراز) — NOT Tehran  
**Dynasty**: Zand dynasty (زندیان), founded 1751  
**Religion**: Twelver Shia Islam  
**Origins**: From the Zand tribe of the Laks (Kurdish/Lur ethnicity)

**Territorial extent (1763)**:
- **Core Persia**: Nearly all of Iran except Khorasan and Balochistan
- **Exclusions**: 
  - **Khorasan**: Held by Shahrokh Shah (Afsharid remnant) in Mashhad as SEPARATE polity
  - **Balochistan**: Not under Zand control
- Did not subdue Khorasan throughout his reign

**Governance style**:
- Deliberately small bureaucracy
- **Personal rule**: Karim Khan personally handled state affairs
- Provincial administration followed Safavid model (*beglerbegis*, *kalantar*, *darugha*, *kadkhuda*)
- Tribal administration created new posts (*ilkhani* for Lur tribes, *ilbegi* for Qashqai tribes)
- **Religious policy**: Unlike Safavids, did not seek *ulama* (clerical) approval for authority

**Character**: Contemporary and historical sources praise Karim Khan for generosity, modesty, and fairness. The Oxford Dictionary of Islam calls him "**the most humane Iranian ruler of the Islamic era**." Wore simple clothing, sat on inexpensive carpet rather than throne.

**Condition of Persia under Karim Khan**: Iran recovered from roughly 40 years of devastation following Nader Shah's death (1747); gained renewed "tranquillity, security, peace, and prosperity"; **peak of Zand rule 1765–1779**.

**1763 Status**: By 1763, Karim Khan had been ruling for 12 years and had consolidated his power. That year (1763), he brought **Agha Mohammad Khan Qajar** and Hosayn Qoli Khan to his capital — the Qajar hostage who would later overthrow the Zand dynasty.

**Economy** (Shiraz-centered):
- **Shiraz**: Political and commercial center; population 40,000–50,000 by late 18th century (surpassing Isfahan)
- **Trade hub**: Attracted foreign traders; British East India Company permitted southern trading post
- **Carpets**: Shiraz, Isfahan, Kerman, Tabriz (Persian carpets world-renowned)
- **Silk**: Gilan province (primary silk-producing region; major export to Russia via Caspian)
- **Dried fruits**: Dates, figs, apricots, raisins, pistachios
- **Persian Gulf port**: Bushehr (primary Gulf port; trade with Oman, India, East Africa)

**Afsharid Khorasan (SEPARATE POLITY in 1763)**:
- **Ruler**: Shahrokh Shah (شاهرخ شاه, 1734–1796; second reign 1750–1796; in power throughout 1763)
- **Capital**: Mashhad (مشهد)
- **Territory**: Western Khorasan (roughly modern Khorasan Province)
- **Status**: Largely a figurehead; real power held by Kurdish, Arab, and Turkic tribal chieftains; effectively a **Durrani client** (Ahmad Shah Durrani invaded multiple times; restored Shahrokh as king 9 May 1755)
- **Legitimacy claim**: Half-Safavid descent (mother was Safavid princess)
- **Death**: 1796, when Agha Mohammad Khan Qajar conquered Khorasan and tortured him to extract Nader's hidden treasures

**MAJOR CHANGE — 1763 vs 1815**:
- **1763**: **Zand dynasty**, capital **Shiraz**, ruled by Karim Khan Zand
- **1815**: **Qajar dynasty**, capital **Tehran**, ruled by Fath-Ali Shah Qajar (r. 1797–1834)
- **Different ruling family, different capital, different governance style**
- Zand dynasty completely destroyed 1794 by Agha Mohammad Khan Qajar

Source: [MONOGRAPH] 1763_WORLD_Ottoman_MENA.md lines 880-1109; [PRIMARY] asia_middleeast_1763.md line 9; [PRIMARY] 1763_DELTA_Ottoman_MENA.md lines 115-164; Wikipedia: Karim Khan Zand; Wikipedia: Zand dynasty; Wikipedia: Shahrokh Shah; The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
