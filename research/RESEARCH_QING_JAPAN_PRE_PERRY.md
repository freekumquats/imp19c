# Qing–Japan Contacts c.1815–1854 (Pre-Perry) — Research Digest

Backing **#81** (Japan tree pre-Perry opening arc). The existing qing_japan_missions.txt covers 1871-1895 only; this arc precedes it (c.1815-1854) and hands off at Perry(1854)/Treaty of Amity(1871).

**Core paradox to dramatize:** Qing China & Tokugawa Japan had NO formal diplomatic relations 1640-1853 — no embassies, no state letters, no tributary tie (Japan deliberately stayed OUTSIDE the Qing tributary order). Contact ran through COMMERCE + INTELLIGENCE only.

**1. Nagasaki trade (唐船 Tōsen / 唐人 Tōjin):** Chinese merchant ships + Dutch VOC the only permitted traders, confined to Nagasaki. Chinese in the walled Tōjin yashiki (唐人屋敷, est. 1689). shinpai (信牌 trust-tally licence, 1715 Shōtoku reforms) capped ships/yr. Japan exported COPPER (dominant — Qing coinage) + tawaramono (sea cucumber/abalone/sharkfin); imported Chinese silk, sugar, medicine, BOOKS. Long decline over period but channel stayed open as live info pipe to Perry.

**2. Fūsetsugaki (風説書) info conduit:** Dutch (阿蘭陀風説書) + Chinese (唐船風説書) REQUIRED to submit written overseas-news reports at Nagasaki = bakufu's main window on the world. Opium War news reached Japan via these; Dutch betsudan fūsetsugaki (別段風説書, from 1840) carried detailed war/Treaty-of-Nanking accounts. Compiled into Mineta Fūkō's Kaigai Shinwa (海外新話, 1849), which bakufu then SUPPRESSED.

**3. Opium War as Japan's cautionary tale (THE key China→Japan link):** China's 1842 defeat shocked Japan. CONCRETE DATABLE PIVOT: 1825 hardline Ikokusen uchiharairei (異国船打払令, repel-foreign-vessels edict) was RESCINDED in 1842 → Tenpō Shinsui kyūyorei (天保薪水給与令, provide-firewood-and-water) EXPLICITLY because of China's defeat. Supercharged kaibō (海防 coastal-defense) + jōi-vs-kaikoku debate.

**4. Wei Yuan's Haiguo Tuzhi (海國圖志):** pub. China 1843(50 juan)/1847(60)/1852(100). Reached Japan via Nagasaki, repeatedly reprinted/excerpted in 1850s Japan, foundational for bakumatsu reformers — MORE influential in Japan than in Qing China. Slogan 師夷長技以制夷.

**5. Ryukyu (琉球) dual subordination (両属 ryōzoku):** tributary of Qing (investiture 冊封 of its kings) AND controlled by Satsuma/Shimazu since 1609 invasion. Only place Qing+Japanese authority physically overlapped pre-Perry → natural friction hook. Bakufu/Satsuma concealed depth of control to keep China tribute-trade. 1844 French naval approach (Alcmène) + British probing made it a 1840s flashpoint (culminates post-period in 1879 Ryukyu shobun annexation).

**6. Wider order:** Qing tributaries = Korea/Ryukyu/Vietnam/Siam, NOT Japan. Japan ran own "four gateways" order (Toby): Nagasaki→China/Dutch, Tsushima(Sō clan)→Korea(waegwan at Pusan), Satsuma→Ryukyu, Matsumae→Ainu. Linked to Qing INDIRECTLY via Korea (itself a Qing tributary). Latent parity tension → 1871 Treaty of Amity (日清修好条規) was first-ever EQUAL Sino-Japanese treaty.

**7. Perry (1853 Uraga / 1854 Convention of Kanagawa 日米和親条約):** Qing barely noticed — consumed by Taiping (Nanjing fell March 1853) + Nian. ASYMMETRY: Japan learned intensely from China's Opium War; China did NOT reciprocally watch Japan's opening. That asymmetry IS the counterfactual lever — a Qing player noticing at all is the divergence.

**Figures/hooks:** CN: Wei Yuan (魏源 1794-1857), Lin Zexu (林則徐), Xu Jiyu (徐繼畬, Yinghuan Zhilüe 瀛環志略 1849). JP reactors: Sakuma Shōzan (佐久間象山), Aizawa Seishisai (会沢正志斎, Shinron 新論 1825), Takashima Shūhan (高島秋帆, 1841 Tokumaru gunnery demo), Yoshida Shōin, Yokoi Shōnan.

**Sources:** Jansen "China in the Tokugawa World" (Harvard 1992, THE key synthesis); Fogel "Articulating the Sinosphere" (2009); Wakabayashi "Anti-Foreignism and Western Learning" (1986); Toby "State and Diplomacy in Early Modern Japan" (1984); Fairbank/Reischauer + Fairbank ed. "The Chinese World Order" (1968).

**8 CANDIDATE MISSION/EVENT BEATS (Qing player):**
1. 唐船 "Tang Ships of Nagasaki" — license/upgrade Tōjin merchant presence → recurring intelligence-on-Japan channel.
2. 別段風説書 "News Travels by Merchant" — Qing chooses to let/spin/suppress the story of its OWN Opium War defeat flowing to Japan.
3. 薪水給與令 "Japan's Cautionary Reversal (1842)" — triggered after Opium War beat; Japan softens expulsion edict because of China's fate → diplomatic opening toward rattled Edo.
4. 海國圖志 "The Book That Woke Japan" — author/disseminate Haiguo Tuzhi; sending copies to Japan spawns reformer chars (fork: cooperation vs accelerated rival Japan). Plugs into Self-Strengthening track.
5. 琉球両属 "Two Masters over Ryukyu" — standing friction: Qing investiture claim vs Satsuma control; escalates on 1840s French/British probing → 3-way.
6. 銅貿易 "Copper for the Cash" — leverage Japanese copper exports as economic wedge toward quasi-official Edo contact.
7. 師夷長技以制夷 "The Barbarian's Own Techniques" — capstone reform translating Opium War lesson into Qing self-strengthening; plugs into existing Self-Strengthening tree.
8. "Before Perry (1853-54 handoff)" — terminal event registering Perry/Kanagawa, transitions into post-Perry/1871 content.
